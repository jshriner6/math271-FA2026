Academic Integrity and the Honor Code.

We learn best together, which is why there is a large amount of collaboration built into our course structure. Artificial intelligence tools can also be extremely useful in learning something new, when used appropriately. However, there is a difference between learning together (or with AI help) and using work that is not your own. If you are wondering if you crossed the line, ask yourself "Could I start over and redo this on my own, and would it basically look like this?" If not, then you are submitting work that is not your own (plagiarism). Copying solutions or statements from the internet without citing your source constitutes plagiarism. We take academic integrity seriously, so all cases of plagiarized work or cheating will receive a 0 and will be submitted to the SRC.
🔗
This should give you some practical ideas of what it looks like to work with peers and use AI well (e.g., copying/pasting a homework statement then copying/pasting the response, versus crafting thoughtful prompts to understand a broad concept, and then checking your understanding by completing a problem without any aid at all). We understand that this is a complex issue, and decisions can often feel grey. Please communicate with me any time you feel unsure about appropriate ways to use your peers and technology for learning course content.

This course will adhere to the CSU Academic Integrity Policy as found in the Colorado State University General Catalog and the Student Conduct Code.
